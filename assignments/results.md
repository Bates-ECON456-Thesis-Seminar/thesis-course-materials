# Results Section Guidelines

Your results section should effectively communicate your key findings to readers. Follow these guidelines to create clear, publication-quality results.

## Tables and Figures

### Tables
- Follow AER style guide formatting (see https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/style-guide)
- Include descriptive titles and detailed captions explaining the contents
- Use readable variable names, not raw code output (e.g. "Unemployment Rate" not "unemp_rate")
- Report standard errors and statistical significance levels consistently
- Include notes explaining any data transformations or sample restrictions
- Limit decimal places to those that are meaningful

Recommended table-making packages:
- Stata: estout, tabout
- R: fixest::etable, stargazer, modelsummary, xtable

### Figures 
- Use appropriate scale and size to clearly show patterns
- Label axes and include units
- Use colorblind-friendly palettes
- Add informative titles and captions
- Consider whether a figure adds value beyond a table

## Narrative Structure

1. Focus on Key Results
- Identify 2-3 main findings that are central to your research question
- Lead with these key results
- Support main findings with additional evidence

2. Tell a Clear Story
- Begin each results subsection with the main takeaway
- Explain how results relate to your research question and hypotheses
- Walk readers through interpretation of coefficients and magnitudes
- Connect findings to economic significance

3. Build Evidence
- Present results in a logical sequence
- Start with main specifications
- Follow with robustness checks and alternative specifications
- Explain how additional results reinforce main findings

## Selection and Organization

- Choose results that directly address your research question
- Include only tables and figures that serve a clear purpose
- Place detailed results in an appendix if they distract from main narrative
- Organize results to build a compelling case for your conclusions
- Remember: Readers can absorb at most 2-3 key findings

Your results section should leave readers with a clear understanding of:
1. What you found
2. Why it matters
3. How confident they should be in the findings
