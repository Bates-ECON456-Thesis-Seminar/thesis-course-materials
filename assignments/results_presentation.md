# Results Presentation Guidelines

## 1. Introduction (Keep It Brief)
- Start by reminding the audience of the topic in one or two sentences.  
- **Example:**  
  *"We analyzed the impact of streetlights on crime rates to understand whether increased lighting reduces criminal activity."*  

## 2. First Slide: Key Findings at a Glance
- Present 3–4 bullet points summarizing the main takeaways.  
- Keep them high-level and easy to grasp.  
- **Example:**  
  - *Crime rates are lower near streetlights.*  
  - *The effect is strongest for property crimes at night.*  
  - *No significant impact was found for violent crimes.*  

## 3. Methods: Keep It Simple
- Provide just enough detail to establish credibility without overwhelming the audience.  
- Mention key data sources and basic analytical approach.  
- **Example:**  
  *"We analyzed 5 years of crime reports, using a difference-in-differences approach to compare crime levels before and after new streetlights were installed."*  

## 4. Main Story: Three Key Exhibits
- Use visuals (charts, maps, or tables) that clearly convey the main insights.  
- Each exhibit should tell part of the story intuitively.  
- **Example Exhibits:**  
  1. A map showing crime density before and after streetlight installation.  
  2. A bar chart comparing crime rates near vs. far from streetlights.  
  3. A trend line showing changes in crime over time.  

## 5. Final Slide: What We Learned
- Recap the main takeaways in 2–3 bullet points.  
- If applicable, suggest implications or next steps.  
- **Example:**  
  - *Lighting improvements can help reduce property crime.*  
  - *Further research is needed to assess long-term effects on violent crime.*  
  - *City planners might consider targeting high-crime areas for future installations.*
